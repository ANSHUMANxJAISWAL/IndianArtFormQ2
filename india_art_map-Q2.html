Still in practice

Five living traditions

These forms weren't retired to museum walls — they're still made and used today. The panels below are small working versions: some animate on their own, some respond to your cursor, one runs off your device's actual clock.

Warli tarpa dance

running simulation

A simplified simulation of the tarpa dance that recurs in Warli wall painting from the hill villages of Maharashtra's Thane and Palghar districts: dancers rendered as triangles and dots, circling a central piper, the way Warli artists compress a whole gathering into a spiral of repeated figures.

Kolam / rangoli tracer

draws with you

Move your cursor or finger across the canvas. Each stroke is mirrored eight ways from the centre, the way a kolam is built from a single repeated gesture around a grid of dots — a threshold drawing practised daily across South India, traditionally in rice flour.

Konark sun-wheel

runs on your clock
--:--local time, stylised — not astronomically exact

Konark's temple wheels double as sundials, each spoke carved so its shadow marks time. This wheel redraws every second from your device's clock: the gnomon's shadow sweeps a full turn every 24 hours.

Pigment basin

mixes as you click

Click the basin to drop natural pigments — lamp-black, vermillion, turmeric, indigo — the palette behind Madhubani line-work, kalamkari dye and Tanjore underpainting alike. Colours diffuse and mix the way ground mineral and plant pigment do in water.

Ajanta by torchlight

follows your cursor

Ajanta's murals were painted and first seen by oil-lamp and torch in a dark cave interior. Move your cursor over the wall to carry a torch across it — left untouched, the torch drifts on its own.
